The studio

One wheel,
one kiln, one pair
of hands.

I'm Kira — a teacher and developer by day, and a potter whenever I can sneak away to the studio. Shiro Studio started because I needed something to do with my hands that had nothing to do with a screen.

Everything is made in my home studio in Burgaw, NC. I throw, trim, glaze, and fire each piece myself with a small Vevor kiln. No two pieces are exactly alike, and that's the point.

Location
Burgaw
Coastal North Carolina
Process
Wheel-thrown
Trimmed, hand-glazed, kiln fired
Materials
Stoneware
& earthenware clay bodies
Batches
Small
Each piece made one at a time
01
Throw
Each piece starts on the wheel — pulled up from a centered lump of clay, shaped by hand and feel.
02
Trim & dry
After drying to leather-hard, the base is trimmed and any attachments — handles, feet — are added.
03
Bisque fire
The first kiln firing burns away organic material and makes the clay hard enough to hold glaze.
04
Glaze & fire
Hand-dipped or brushed in original glaze recipes, then fired to cone 6. The kiln does the rest.
"

There's something grounding about making things with your hands when your whole workday lives on a screen. Clay doesn't care about your code.

— Kira Shinn · Teacher, developer, potter
